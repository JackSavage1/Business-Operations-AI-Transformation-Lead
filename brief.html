One example, start to finish: members missing their visits

A common problem for a company that schedules visits for health plan members. This is an illustration, not your data.

Full dayThe clinician sees a confirmed schedule.

Where it breaks: after the reminder goes out, nobody owns the follow-up.

What changed: every visit gets confirmed, and the people most likely to miss get a human call. The scheduling system stays the same.

What I would ask first

  • Out of every 10 booked visits, how many don't happen?
  • Who finds out first when a member can't make it?
  • What happens to that open slot today?

How I would prove it worked

  • Run it in one market for six weeks; keep a similar market as the comparison.
  • Track one number: the share of booked visits that actually happen.
  • Illustration: going from 1 in 5 visits missed to 1 in 8 means more members seen by the same clinicians.

Where AI helps, with a person in charge

I use AI for the repetitive parts of the work and keep people on the decisions. Member privacy comes first: only vendors with a signed BAA, only the information the task needs, and a record of what the AI did.

Call notes

After each call, AI drafts the note and the next step. The agent reviews it in one click, so less time goes to typing after every call.

anything health-related or urgent always goes to a nurse, never automated.

Reminders and follow-up

Automation sends confirmations and lists the members who didn't reply, so schedulers spend their time on calls that matter.

schedulers decide who to call and how to help.

Weekly reporting

A dashboard updates itself instead of someone rebuilding a spreadsheet every Monday. Managers start the week from what is off track.

each team agrees on what the numbers mean.

My first 90 days

Days 1–30 · Learn
  • Meet every department lead and their frontline team
  • Shadow scheduling, the call center, and chart review
  • Agree on the handful of numbers that matter most
  • Build a ranked list of the biggest pain points
Days 31–60 · First wins
  • Ship two small fixes that teams feel right away
  • Start one AI pilot alongside the current process, checked by people
  • Send a short weekly update on what changed
Days 61–90 · Prove it
  • Compare the pilot against the comparison group
  • Train teams with a simple one-page guide
  • Decide together: expand it, adjust it, or stop it
  • Set the next set of priorities
